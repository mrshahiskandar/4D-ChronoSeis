How it works

1. Inputs. Choose the two seismic surveys, the well markers, well folder and platforms, and a working directory.

2. Run. The 4D ChronoSeis engine runs on the HPC in Jupyter, stage by stage: match the surveys, compute the difference, pick horizons from well markers, measure amplitude change along each horizon, and classify hardening, softening and unswept areas.

3. Summary. A plain-language view: which horizons changed, how confident we are, and possible untapped areas where oil or gas may have been missed.

4. Detail. Maps of every horizon and attribute in real X/Y coordinates, statistics and all figures.

Gemma (the AI model hosted on the HPC) explains results and answers questions. It only sees the computed numbers.

Interpretations are hypotheses. Confirm with well logs, rock physics, pressure and production data.
